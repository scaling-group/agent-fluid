# Productive-closure cadence candidate

## Evidence and visual diagnosis before editing

- All four sampled rollouts satisfy the frozen contract: direct uniform
  initialization in still water, `U_infinity=(0,0,0)`, no cylinders, no
  prewarm, finite dynamics, and `capture` termination. Their policies and
  combined keyframe sheets are byte-identical, so they are four replications
  of one controller rather than four mechanism comparisons.
- The top-down row shows self-propelled motion with a coherent alternating
  red/blue street. The fish initially travels nearly left while the target is
  down-left, then develops a smooth target-signed arc and crosses the capture
  circle. The oblique row confirms compact three-dimensional Lambda2
  structures shed behind the posterior body without visible wake collapse.
  Metrics agree: capture occurs at `26.411 T` and `0.7496 L`, mean/max speed is
  about `0.501/0.667 L/T`, and sampled local flow remains small relative to
  body speed. The motion is generated by the fish, not ambient advection.
- The distance integral, not success class, is now the available improvement
  axis. During the first `6 T`, distance falls only from `12.3277 L` to about
  `11.3249 L` while closing rises from near zero to about `0.328 L/T`. At
  `8.003 T`, body-frame target angle is about `35 deg`, closing has fallen to
  about `0.215 L/T`, and the large-error redirect is active. The inherited
  controller's extra cadence term is largest when closing is poor, so it can
  add propulsive authority during redirection rather than reserve it for
  already productive translation.
- There is no distinct failure keyframe in the current sampled set. The most
  informative failure contrast is therefore the inherited capture-turn-hold
  rollout documented by the assigned parent: it retained a strong alternating
  three-dimensional wake but missed at `1.3896 L`, accelerated to about
  `1.450 L/T`, and exited the domain. The inherited terminal-relief candidate
  also missed at `1.2329 L`, while a speed-envelope guard delayed an otherwise
  equivalent capture. Together these results make global thrust increases,
  terminal cadence branches, and protective speed attenuation poor next
  tests; a new drive schedule must be alignment- and progress-gated and leave
  the proven redirect untouched.

## Policy hypothesis

Preserve the completion-gated C-bend, posterior-lag state-feedback carrier,
route curvature, approach schedule, and half-cycle steering. Change only the
small progress-dependent cadence add-on: instead of increasing cadence as
closing becomes deficient, earn the same bounded allowance from the product
of positive closing confidence and normalized body-frame target alignment.
The pre-existing far-distance and turn-load gates remain in series, so this
does not create a terminal burst or add drive during a large redirect.

Expected result: retain the sampled target arc and coherent wake while putting
the optional cadence authority on productive segments, improving capture time
or the `2.6134 L` distance-integral term without increasing the allowance's
maximum magnitude. Falsify the mechanism if capture is lost or delayed, mean
distance does not improve, the early arc moves farther from the target, the
wake loses coherence, or speed, load, and action materially exceed the
replicated parent envelope.
