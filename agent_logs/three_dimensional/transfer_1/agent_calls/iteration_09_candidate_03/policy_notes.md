# Phase-neutral guidance with steering-preserving allocation

## Evidence and visual diagnosis before editing

- All four sampled rollouts satisfy the direct-uniform still-water contract:
  `U_infinity=(0,0,0)`, no cylinders, no prewarm, finite dynamics, and
  `capture`.  Two samples reproduce the progress-gated posterior controller
  exactly at `26.0425 T`, score `-0.69471683`, and distance integral
  `2.59751 L`; no sampled semantic failure is available.  The inherited
  guidance supplies the earlier informative failure topology: response-only
  redirect release passed above-left at `2.4625 L` and exited left, while
  completion-gated release converted that route to capture.  The redirect and
  posterior carrier therefore remain protected mechanisms.
- I inspected the combined top-down vorticity and oblique Lambda2 sheets for
  the strongest carrier-first-projection capture, the reproduced posterior
  baseline, and the phase-neutral-yaw capture.  All three are visibly
  self-propelled from quiescent water: compact startup structures grow into an
  alternating posterior sheet, the wake bends with the broad target-signed
  route, and coherent three-dimensional packets persist into capture.  The
  phase-neutral rollout takes a more aggressive terminal arc; its larger,
  more discrete late packets agree with its higher speed and action-limit
  residence rather than establishing a superior wake by appearance alone.
- The sampled carrier-first projection is the strongest complete result.  By
  projecting the rhythmic carrier before adding target steering, it improves
  the posterior baseline from `26.0425` to `25.9545 T`, distance integral from
  `2.59751` to `2.55008 L`, and score from `-0.69472` to `-0.64779`.  It also
  cuts any-joint acceleration-limit residence from `84.35%` to `33.95%` and
  peak speed from `0.7170` to `0.6672 L/T`, with unchanged sampled peak
  force/moment coefficients near `0.02974/0.01484`.  Its visible carrier and
  target-directed route remain coherent, so this allocation must be retained.
- The separate phase-neutral-yaw controller changes guidance semantics rather
  than carrier gains: it estimates gait-locked body yaw from observed head-
  joint rate before the target-relative rate loop.  Relative to the posterior
  baseline it reaches capture much earlier (`25.0745 T`), lowers the distance
  integral to `2.55414 L`, and improves score to `-0.65392`.  Its cost is
  `89.41%` any-joint limit residence, `0.7806 L/T` peak speed, and a slightly
  worse integral than carrier-first projection.  This makes phase rejection a
  useful route mechanism but not an independently acceptable actuator result.

## One-candidate policy hypothesis

Start from the evaluated carrier-first-projection capture and add only the
sampled joint-state phase rejection to its guidance module.  Recent body yaw
is decomposed into a gait-synchronous estimate from `phi_dot[1]` and a slower
route response; only the corrected response enters target turn-rate feedback
and redirect release.  Carrier-first projection then preserves bounded target
steering authority without inheriting the phase-neutral candidate's practice
of summing steering underneath a saturated carrier.  This is one compact
combination of two separately positive mechanisms; it adds no clock, global
direction, route, target identity, or scalar propulsion gain.

Expected evidence is earlier capture than the projection parent while keeping
its smooth coherent wake, low distance integral, and actuator/load envelope.
Falsify the combination if capture is lost or later than `25.9545 T`, distance
integral exceeds `2.55008 L`, any-joint limit residence returns materially
toward `89.41%`, peak speed approaches or exceeds `0.7806 L/T`, peak load grows,
or the target-signed arc/wake coherence regresses.  This candidate has no
same-worker CFD evidence; formal evaluation occurs after exit.
