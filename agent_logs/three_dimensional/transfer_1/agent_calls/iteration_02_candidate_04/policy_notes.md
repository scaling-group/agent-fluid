# Wake-policy candidate diagnosis

## Evidence read before editing

- The sampled rollouts all satisfy the experiment contract: direct uniform
  quiescent initialization with `U_infinity=(0,0,0)`, no cylinders, no
  prewarm, finite dynamics, and the inertial moving-window transport. Motion
  and wake advection are therefore generated by the swimmer rather than by an
  imposed current.
- The best finite rollout is `solver_6c46616730e6`. Its top-down row shows a
  strong alternating vortex street and its oblique Lambda2 row shows compact
  three-dimensional structures persisting to termination. Distance falls
  from `12.328L` to `6.067L`, but then reopens to `10.494L`; heading grows from
  `0.506` to `1.696` rad and the fish repeats the inherited lower-boundary exit
  at `25.795T`. The course-error residual improves the seed minimum by only
  `0.060L` and does not change termination or trajectory topology, so it is
  not evidence that the branch-confounded steering structure improved.
- The inherited parent note predicted that direct body-bearing-to-posterior-
  mean-curvature steering would reverse the lower-exit drift. Its evaluated
  candidate `solver_f0f4cd87e65f` falsifies that prediction: the top-down and
  oblique sheets show an immediate tight clockwise route with a coherent but
  route-wasting wake, heading falls from `0.506` to `-1.432` rad, minimum
  distance is only `12.206L`, and the fish exits the upper boundary at
  `7.794T`. The lower-amplitude yaw-damped mean-curvature candidate
  `solver_b0e1dc12d96c` repeats the upper-exit class at `9.086T` with a
  `12.272L` minimum. Thus the earlier correlation between cumulative tail
  tangent and heading did not identify usable closed-loop steering authority.
- The propulsive carrier remains the reusable part of the evidence. The seed
  and `6c466...` retain coherent wakes and close about `6.2L`; both slow/static
  mean-curvature variants close less than `0.13L`. Increasing amplitude or
  cadence is therefore not the missing first mechanism.
- Direct yaw-rate feedback is not clean enough to be the sole response signal:
  the seven-step `turn_rate_recent` window follows tail-beat yaw and reaches
  roughly `+/-2--3 rad/T` even during the useful closing segment. A tight
  tanh servo around it would alternate at the carrier phase and risk persistent
  command switching. Body-frame velocity course varies on the route scale and
  directly reports the cross-target translation that preceded the miss.

## One candidate mechanism

Preserve the observed state-feedback oscillator, posterior lag, and bounded
distance/progress cadence scheduling. Replace all inherited mean-tail,
direction-specific recovery, and competing direct-steer branches with one
reflection-symmetric course-response curvature servo. At low speed it uses
the normalized body-frame target angle so steering is defined from release.
As speed becomes informative, it continuously blends to target-angle minus
achieved body-frame velocity-course angle. The bounded error shifts both joint
accelerations in the locally observed sign convention, with greater posterior
share, while oscillatory phase remains entirely in joint state.

The response variable makes the translation materially different from a
persistent bearing-to-static-tail offset: the initial positive target angle
requests a positive shared joint bias and a clockwise correction, but the
command decays or reverses as achieved velocity course crosses the target
course. Expected evidence is retained alternating propulsion, early closure,
and neither the `f0f4...` upper-exit overshoot nor the `6c46...` lower-exit
drift. Reject this mechanism if it fails to beat the `6.067L` minimum, repeats
either boundary-exit topology, collapses the coherent wake, or creates
persistent route-scale action saturation.

## Non-CFD algebraic checks

- Mirrored release probes at `target_body_L=(-12.18,+/-1.91)` produce mirrored
  `turn_command=+/-0.451`; the corresponding posterior steering contribution
  is `+/-5.413 rad/T^2`. The complete initial actions remain inside the fixed
  `31.416 rad/T^2` acceleration envelope.
- With the positive target angle held fixed, an achieved course above the
  target changes the command from `+0.451` to `-0.814`, while an achieved
  course below it gives `+0.971`. This establishes reflection and release/
  reversal semantics in the policy algebra only; it is not CFD evidence that
  the shared acceleration bias generates the intended hydrodynamic turn.
