# Speed-gated posterior lag-duty candidate

## Evidence diagnosis before policy edit

All four sampled rollouts report direct uniform still-water initialization,
`U_infinity=(0,0,0)`, no cylinders, and no prewarm. Motion and visible wakes
are therefore generated by the swimmer rather than ambient advection.

- `solver_b6bb94d9cdaf` is the best finite sample by score (`-7.6367`) and
  mean distance (`6.5015L`). Its top-down row shows sustained leftward
  translation and a coherent alternating vortex street; the oblique row shows
  organized three-dimensional Lambda2 structures through termination. It
  reaches `5.3570L`, then turns upward and exits at center
  `(8.6493,15.2024)L`. The centered joint-state oscillator and posterior lag
  are useful; additive bearing curvature plus short-window yaw braking does
  not settle the route.
- `solver_e450df1efa49`, the prefilled solver parent, has the cleanest long
  axial wake and the best closest pass (`4.1281L`). It remains above the target
  row, passes it, and exits left at center `y=12.4806L`, with final distance
  `9.1538L`. Its raw accelerations exceed the physical envelope on about
  `71.3%/68.4%` of trace rows, so its propulsion is worth preserving but its
  layered static-curvature controller and actuator usage are not.
- `solver_59bc4ebdddec` retains an organized propulsive wake and monotonically
  reaches `7.5311L`, but bearing-rate release of a signed mean curvature still
  produces the same upper-boundary topology at `14.65T`. Releasing curvature
  magnitude without changing the actuator primitive is not enough.
- `solver_b43b85a4f60c` directly falsifies the inherited next-step hypothesis
  of posterior amplitude half-cycle asymmetry. Its top-down row shows little
  translation followed by a tight upward curl, and its oblique row contains
  only a short local wake instead of the long organized street in the useful
  samples. It reaches only `11.9932L`, has mean distance `12.2454L`, and exits
  high at center `(19.9396,15.2014)L` after `8.591T`. The controller commands
  full target-angle steering at release and scales the `q1` term by as much as
  `34%`; that combination fails to establish propulsion before redirecting it.

The selected policy keeps the evidenced centered anterior oscillator and base
posterior velocity lag. It replaces static curvature and direct posterior
amplitude scaling with one new actuator primitive: the persistent body-frame
bearing modulates posterior *velocity lag* on the compatible observed
`phi_dot[1]` half-cycle. A continuous forward-speed gate withholds this
steering until self-propulsion appears; it is observation feedback, not a
clock or hidden stage. The construction is reflection-equivariant because
bearing and the joint-velocity phase side reverse together. It preserves a
positive posterior lag on both half-cycles and smoothly bounds both commands.

Expected result: first establish the coherent wake seen in the useful samples,
then create a bounded cycle-mean bend through lag duty rather than amplitude or
static offset, moving the path down from the sampled upper/above-target routes.
Falsify the mechanism if the long wake and forward speed do not develop, the
trajectory again curls out near release or exits high, closest distance does
not beat `5.3570L`, or joint/command-limit residence worsens. No sampled policy
entered the `0.75L` capture neighborhood, so terminal scheduling is not used.
