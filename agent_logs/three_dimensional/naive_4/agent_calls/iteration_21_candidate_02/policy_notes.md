# Wake-policy diagnosis and candidate hypothesis

## Prior evidence

- All four sampled evaluations are valid direct-uniform still-water rollouts
  (`U_infinity=[0,0,0]`, no prewarm) and capture at about `16.0545T` after
  `239` moving-window shifts. The top-down sheets show self-propelled target
  approach behind a coherent alternating vorticity street; the oblique
  Lambda2 sheets show the same compact three-dimensional alternating wake
  without visible instability or loss of body-wave organization.
- The two copies of the assigned-parent corridor/escape policy are exactly
  repeatable at final distance `0.746953L`, distance integral `1.930772L`, and
  score `-0.048053`. The older instantaneous bearing-rate escape variant is
  trajectory-equivalent at `0.746955L`, `1.930773L`, and `-0.048055`.
- The strongest finite sample changes only the supplemental-yaw release: a
  smooth small-bearing, closing-approach turnaround gate preserves the wake,
  milestones, `239` shifts, and essentially identical arrival time, while
  improving final distance to `0.746212L`, distance integral to `1.930147L`,
  and score to `-0.047281`. Thus cruise propulsion and steering should be
  inherited from this sample, while the remaining opportunity is terminal
  crossing geometry rather than more propulsion or broad redirect authority.
- In the inherited terminal reconstruction, projected miss and closing speed
  improve during the final `0.0715T`, but bearing reopens and target-signed
  measured yaw grows. Releasing supplemental curvature helps, yet it bottoms
  out at zero extra correction and cannot oppose excess yaw generated by the
  still-active base bend. Another release threshold would repeat the last
  lineage's trajectory-near-equivalent edits instead of testing a new action.

## Candidate

Start from the best sampled small-bearing turnaround release and add one new
mechanism: bounded posterior mean-curvature yaw damping. It is active only when
the fish is closing inside the predicted capture corridor, the measured yaw is
target-signed, and the body-frame bearing magnitude is reopening. The damping
opposes normalized measured yaw and modifies both allocation branches, so it
changes feasible posterior action rather than wrapping a hard clamp or merely
releasing an optional term. The state-feedback oscillator, posterior traveling
wave, base redirect, wave relief, and anterior corridor behavior remain intact.

Expected test: pre-terminal milestones and both-view wake topology remain
unchanged, while the last fraction of a beat has less excess yaw, no later
capture, and a lower final-distance/distance-integral cost than the sampled
`-0.047281` policy. Reject the mechanism if it activates outside the safe
closing corridor, delays or loses capture, worsens the crossing or distance
integral, raises limiting/load peaks materially, or disrupts the coherent wake.

## Pre-evaluation checks

- Replaying both policies on the best sample's recorded states changes only
  `16` posterior commands, from `15.9719T`/`0.8361L` through capture. The first
  difference is less than `1e-5 rad/T^2`; the largest is a bounded
  `4.30 rad/T^2` at crossing. This is an action-support check, not a claim
  about the unevaluated closed-loop trajectory.
- The policy returned finite two-joint accelerations for the contract state and
  for a non-finite-observation fallback. One thousand paired reflected states
  produced equal-and-opposite accelerations to numerical tolerance, confirming
  that the added target/yaw response preserves reflection equivariance.
