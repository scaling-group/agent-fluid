# Wake-policy candidate notes

## Inherited and sampled evidence

- The assigned parent is the strongest sampled first-generation controller. It
  keeps the naive joint-state oscillator and adds a `12 deg` bounded posterior
  mean-curvature target from body-frame bearing plus a short bearing trend.
  Its direct-uniform still-water rollout is self-propelled and reduces distance
  from `12.328L` to `9.141L`, reaching `0.753U` and lasting `13.129T`. Both the
  top-down vorticity row and oblique Lambda2 row show a coherent alternating 3D
  wake from roughly `4T` through termination.
- That improvement is not target control. The parent crosses from positive to
  negative target bearing near `8T`, its center continues upward from about
  `y=14.06L` to `15.201L`, and it exits the upper virtual boundary with bearing
  about `-1.33 rad`. Its joint rates touch the `260 deg/T` envelope in about 9%
  of samples and a raw acceleration request exceeds `1800 deg/T^2` in about 81%
  of samples, so more carrier gain is not supported.
- The other two posterior-only curvature variants also retain a clear 3D wake
  but repeat the same centerline-crossing and upper-exit topology: the
  bearing-trend variant reaches only `11.642L` before exit at `9.350T`, and the
  bearing-plus-instantaneous-yaw-rate variant reaches `11.824L` before exit at
  `9.191T`. Thus gain, scale, trend-lead, and rate-feedback differences within
  a static mean-tail offset did not improve termination class.
- The most informative failure biases both the oscillator center and posterior
  center. Its combined sheet shows little organized wake through `4T`, followed
  by a broad upward hook; quantitatively the head oscillation remains within
  about `8 deg`, distance worsens to `13.403L`, and the center exits at
  `(20.993,15.200)L`. Although that architecture removes acceleration clipping,
  it also removes useful forward travel. Later policies should not move the
  Van der Pol head center until propulsion can be preserved independently.
- The inherited first-generation notes hypothesized that bounded target-to-mean
  curvature would prevent the seed's hook. The four completed samples now
  narrow that claim: posterior-only curvature is directionally useful for
  progress, but static offset variants did not recover after alignment was
  crossed, and anterior mean-centering was actively harmful.

All four diagnostics confirm `U_infinity=(0,0,0)`, direct uniform initialization,
no cylinders, and no prewarm. The motion and wakes are therefore generated by
the candidate policies rather than ambient advection or an inherited flow.

## Policy hypothesis

Replace the static posterior mean offset with one phase-aware steering
primitive: bounded half-cycle amplitude asymmetry of the existing lagged tail
target. Body-frame bearing plus its observed short-window trend selects the
turn direction. The posterior half-cycle bending in that direction is
strengthened while the opposite half-cycle is weakened; a smooth joint-state
phase proxy and bounded tail target keep the mapping continuous. The anterior
oscillator and its center remain unchanged.

This should preserve the parent's coherent traveling wake and forward speed
while producing a cycle-integrated corrective moment that switches sides after
the bearing crossing, instead of asking a static tail offset to fight yaw
inertia throughout both halves of the beat. Evidence should show bounded or
recovering bearing after the first crossing, lower upward drift, and a later or
better termination while retaining meaningful distance progress. Falsify this
translation if it loses the alternating wake, materially increases angle/rate
limit occupancy, repeats the upper-boundary exit with diverging negative
bearing, or fails to beat the parent's `9.141L` closest approach or termination
class.
