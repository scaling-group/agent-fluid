# Wake-policy candidate notes

## Evidence diagnosis before editing

- I read the assigned parent guidance, the four sampled solver results, and the
  inherited optimizer logs before proposing this candidate. Every completed
  rollout used direct-uniform still water (`U_infinity=(0,0,0)`), no cylinders,
  and no prewarm snapshot, so its motion and alternating wake are generated by
  the swimmer rather than advection or moving-window transport.
- I inspected both the top-down mid-plane vorticity row and the oblique
  body/Lambda2 row for the `2.4431L` strongest sample, the `2.8455L`
  distance-relief failure, and the inherited `3.2805L` course-angle failure.
  All three visibly retain a traveling three-dimensional wake and turn down and
  left, but none redirects before crossing below the target; each ultimately
  exits the lower virtual boundary. The strongest sample maintains mean body
  speed `0.669U` while local flow averages only `0.019U`, reaches its minimum at
  `17.869T`, and then powers away to `9.1933L`, consistent with a controlled
  pass-by rather than passive flow transport.
- The inherited course-angle replacement is a concrete negative result. It
  preserves a coherent wake and survives to `31.917T`, but lowers mean speed to
  `0.618U`, misses by `3.2805L`, finishes at `9.5756L`, and still exits below.
  Instantaneous body-frame velocity therefore does not provide the slow course
  signal hypothesized by its parent; it contains substantial tailbeat slip and
  should not replace yaw feedback for this gait.
- Full target direction remains the correct geometry: the acute adapter bearing
  folds target-ahead and target-behind states, while the full body-frame angle
  grows past `pi/2` after the pass. Yet the completed full-direction-only policy
  reached `2.4939L` and retained the same bottom-exit topology, so correcting
  that semantic alias alone acts too late.
- The actionable signal is the gait contamination inside measured yaw rate.
  Through the target-ahead portion (`t <= 18T`) of all four sampled traces,
  `heading_rate` is strongly anticorrelated with anterior joint rate (about
  `-0.65 * phi_dot1` for the best carrier). The raw rate has standard deviation
  about `2.0 rad/T`, exceeds `2 rad/T` for roughly `40%` of samples, and makes
  `direction - 0.18*heading_rate` oppose target direction about `22%` of the
  time. The body-frame residual `heading_rate + 0.65*phi_dot1` has standard
  deviation about `0.6 rad/T`, never exceeds `2 rad/T` in those completed
  traces, and cuts the wrong-sign fraction to about `6%`. The relation also
  survives the inherited course-angle trajectory, whereas raw anterior
  acceleration is already clamped for `71--75%` of samples. This supports a
  feedback-architecture change rather than more curvature or command ceiling.

## Candidate hypothesis

Preserve the strongest sampled `0.55T`, 28-degree joint-state oscillator,
restrained 7-degree full-anterior curvature, posterior lag/gating, and
`28 rad/T^2` reserve. Use full signed target direction for target-ahead/behind
semantics, but replace raw yaw-rate damping with one joint-phase-compensated
rate: `heading_rate + joint_yaw_rate_coupling * phi_dot1`. This removes the
repeatable tailbeat-scale rigid-body yaw component before moving the carrier's
mean curvature, without adding a clock, hidden state, route, stronger static
bend, or raw velocity-course command.

The expected signature is the same early self-propelled wake and progress as
the `2.4431L` comparator, fewer beat-scale reversals of requested curvature,
and a sustained pre-pass redirect that yields capture, a smaller miss, or at
least a better termination topology. Falsify the mechanism if early propulsion
or wake continuity weakens, the joint/yaw relation changes enough that the
residual remains oscillatory, the same lower-boundary pass-by persists, a tight
curl replaces it, or joint-rate/command saturation and loads worsen.
