## Policy hypothesis

Preserve the parent's successful route responses and replace only the yaw-only
steering gate with a normalized wake-event coincidence gate. Measure local
relative crossflow at the head, scale it by the sampled RMS order, and multiply
its magnitude ratio by the existing normalized yaw-load ratio. Strongly reduce
the target-steering residual only when both cues are large; retain the existing
nonzero authority floor and never gate the base traveling wave. This should
keep rejection during coupled crossflow/yaw events while avoiding withdrawal
of route authority during large gait-generated moment alone.

Expected evidence is preserved first-crossing capture and diagonal wake-entry
topology, with arrival/mean distance no worse than `44.121/1.70618L` and no
increase in force, moment, or effort. Reject the detector if capture is lost,
the lower excursion deepens, loads rise, or the route regresses even if command
energy falls. The crossflow scale is a fixed-snapshot calibration hypothesis,
not a universal wake constant; changed-wake robustness requires later evidence.
