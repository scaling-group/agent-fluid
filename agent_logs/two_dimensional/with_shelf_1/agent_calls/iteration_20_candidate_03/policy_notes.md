# Candidate diagnosis and hypothesis

## Evidence read before architecture

- The shared prewarm sheet confirms the common held-fish initial condition:
  four developed, interacting vortex streets lie between the fish and target.
  The released sheets for all four sampled policies show the same behavior: a
  sharp clockwise redirect, a sustained self-propelled leftward traverse, and
  first-entry capture from downstream. The fish is not merely advected because
  its mean upstream displacement rate exceeds the sampled mean local upstream
  flow. No sampled termination failure is available, so inherited domain-exit
  and instability results bound this edit textually rather than visually.
- The coherent bearing-window parent reaches in `34.7105`, with `1.62283L`
  mean distance, `46985.9` total command energy, and `68.96/1036.40`
  force/moment RMS. Targetward relative-crossflow release improves these to
  `33.9460`, `1.60066L`, and `46092.2`, but raises loads to
  `85.04/1244.16`; it changes response on the same route, not the corridor.
- The assigned force-confirmed relative-crossflow parent is the best scalar
  sample (`0.278591`) and reaches in `33.7260` with `1.59412L` mean distance
  and `45776.3` total energy. It does not deliver its proposed unloading:
  force/moment RMS rise again to `93.64/1383.73`, about `10.1%/11.2%` above
  crossflow-only, while joint-speed and command limits remain touched. The
  multiplication of crossflow response by co-signed force support can only
  reduce environmental-assistance credit and restore optional burst, so its
  faster arrival and higher loads are consistent with increased authority.
- Targetward local-flow replacement also reaches (`33.9900`) but produces
  `94.55/1395.74` force/moment RMS. Inherited heading-rate, previous-command,
  and direct-force replacements regress route or load. These results reject
  another kinematic proxy, local/relative-flow swap, force credential, or
  scalar gain edit while preserving the proven carrier and route controller.

## One candidate hypothesis

Preserve the oscillator, posterior lag, raw-bearing mean steering and reserve,
course-slip damping, base half-cycle asymmetry, targetward relative-crossflow
release, assisting-moment response, and coherent joint-speed release. Change
only the role of lateral force: its absolute normalized magnitude is load
pressure, not evidence that crossflow assists the route. Combine that pressure
with crossflow release so either helpful wake transport or high lateral load
withdraws only the optional redirect burst. Opposing flow retains redirect
authority unless measured load is already high; the base asymmetry, mean
steering, and propulsion carrier remain intact.

This is falsified by lost capture, material arrival/mean-distance regression
without a load benefit, or force/moment RMS not falling materially below the
force-confirmed parent. A successful same-prewarm evaluation would still not
establish changed-phase or layout robustness.
